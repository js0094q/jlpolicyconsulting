# Site Style System
## JL Policy Consulting Website

## Objective

Maintain a consistent visual and structural style across the entire JL Policy Consulting website.

This document defines the default site-wide design and content rules that all pages should follow unless a page-specific spec explicitly overrides them.

The website should feel:
- authoritative,
- restrained,
- premium,
- analytical,
- policy-literate,
- commercially credible.

The site should not feel:
- oversized,
- trendy,
- startup-generic,
- over-designed,
- agency-like,
- content-heavy without hierarchy,
- visually loud.

This style system exists to ensure that new pages, redesigns, and refinements preserve the same overall design language and information architecture discipline.

---

## Core Design Principle

The site should present serious policy and reimbursement expertise with a clean editorial interface.

Design should support:
- clarity,
- hierarchy,
- credibility,
- reading comfort,
- trust,
- conversion.

Design should not compete with the content.

---

## Global Visual Direction

### Keep
- restrained palette,
- clean backgrounds,
- strong typography,
- editorial spacing,
- subtle polish,
- minimal visual noise.

### Avoid
- oversized typography,
- dramatic hero sections,
- excessive visual effects,
- unnecessary gradients,
- decorative motion,
- cluttered cards,
- oversized iconography,
- template-like “consulting” sections,
- bloated page intros.

---

## Typography System

Typography should be controlled and consistent across the site.

### Desktop target ranges
- Primary page hero H1: `40px` to `48px`
- Secondary page H1: `36px` to `44px`
- Section H2: `28px` to `36px`
- Section H3 / card titles: `20px` to `26px`
- Body copy: `16px` to `18px`
- Small labels / eyebrow text: `12px` to `14px`
- Supporting metadata text: `13px` to `15px`

### Rules
- Do not use oversized display typography.
- Keep headlines visually strong but controlled.
- Constrain text width for readability.
- Avoid large multi-line headlines unless structurally necessary.
- Body text should be readable and calm, not dense and not oversized.
- Use type to create hierarchy, not drama.

---

## Spacing System

Spacing should feel premium and deliberate, but not inflated.

### Rules
- Section spacing should be generous but controlled.
- Do not create large vertical gaps just to make the page feel expensive.
- Reduce padding when sections are text-heavy.
- Use tighter spacing when sections serve as transitions.
- Cards should have breathing room without looking oversized.
- Footer spacing should be compact relative to hero and content sections.

### Priority
Whitespace should clarify structure, not create emptiness.

---

## Layout Rules

### General layout
- Use constrained content widths.
- Prefer clean grid structures.
- Keep text blocks readable in width.
- Avoid full-width text-heavy sections without containment.
- Maintain alignment consistency across sections.

### Preferred patterns
- single-column editorial sections for prose,
- two-column sections when comparison or support content is helpful,
- 3- or 4-card grids for structured consulting topics,
- compact proof or metric bands,
- restrained CTA sections.

### Avoid
- too many equal-weight sections,
- page layouts that feel like stacked marketing modules,
- visual inconsistency between pages,
- unnecessary alternation just for variety.

---

## Page Structure Rules

All major pages should follow a coherent structure.

### Default page model
1. Header
2. Intro / hero
3. Core value or page purpose
4. Supporting structured sections
5. Proof, examples, or authority content where relevant
6. CTA
7. Footer

### Rules
- Every page should have a clear primary purpose.
- Every page should have a readable narrative sequence.
- Sections should not feel interchangeable.
- Pages should not open with oversized generic intros.
- Pages should not feel like a long list of blocks with equal weight.

---

## Hero Rules

Hero sections must be tight and controlled.

### Hero must do
- identify the page purpose,
- orient the visitor,
- establish relevance,
- provide a next step.

### Hero must not do
- explain everything,
- dominate the entire screen,
- use long generic paragraphs,
- rely on huge typography for impact.

### Preferred hero content
- eyebrow or context label,
- concise headline,
- short supporting paragraph,
- one primary CTA,
- optional secondary CTA,
- optional compact proof or reference panel.

---

## Section Rules

Each section must have a defined job.

### Acceptable section purposes
- explain a consulting area,
- frame a problem,
- establish differentiation,
- provide proof,
- highlight selected content,
- support conversion.

### Section rules
- every section needs a clear reason to exist,
- headings should be specific,
- body copy should be concise,
- avoid repeating the same message in multiple sections,
- do not use sections as filler.

---

## Cards and Content Blocks

Cards should be structured and useful, not decorative.

### Cards should include
- clear title,
- concise explanatory copy,
- optional link or CTA,
- clear differentiation between cards.

### Cards should not
- act as vague keyword containers,
- repeat the same generic format endlessly,
- rely on decorative styling to create meaning,
- become too dense with text.

### Preferred card types
- consulting area cards,
- insight or research previews,
- proof statements,
- topic summaries.

---

## Copy Density Rules

The site should be text-rich enough to feel substantive, but not crowded.

### Prefer
- short paragraphs,
- clear headings,
- grouped related ideas,
- concise support copy,
- strong scannability.

### Avoid
- long generic intros,
- paragraph overload,
- repeated phrases,
- overly broad business language,
- résumé-like dumping of credentials.

### General rule
Every sentence should either:
- clarify expertise,
- define a problem,
- show value,
- provide proof,
- move the visitor forward.

If it does not do one of those, it is a candidate for removal.

---

## Consulting Page Rules

Consulting pages should feel structured and decision-oriented.

### Must communicate
- what the consulting area is,
- what problems it addresses,
- who it is for,
- what decisions it informs,
- why the perspective is useful.

### Avoid
- giant lists of capabilities,
- shallow service blurbs,
- generic advisory language.

### Prefer
- grouped service logic,
- commercially relevant framing,
- concise specificity,
- clear problem-to-value flow.

---

## About Page Rules

The About page should build trust without reading like a résumé dump.

### Must communicate
- professional perspective,
- domain fluency,
- analytical orientation,
- practical relevance.

### Avoid
- chronological biography overload,
- inflated personal branding language,
- generic mission language.

### Prefer
- concise positioning,
- selective experience framing,
- clear explanation of approach,
- connection between expertise and client value.

---

## Insights and Research Rules

Insights and research pages should feel editorial and analytical.

### Must communicate
- seriousness,
- quality,
- specificity,
- analytical depth.

### Prefer
- clean article cards,
- strong metadata hierarchy,
- restrained previews,
- easy scanning,
- clear relationship between insight and research content.

### Avoid
- cluttered listings,
- oversized previews,
- too many homepage-style promotional treatments.

---

## CTA Rules

Calls to action should be clear and restrained.

### Preferred CTA types
- View Consulting
- Read Insights
- Explore Research
- Contact Us

### Rules
- one primary CTA per section,
- avoid too many competing buttons,
- avoid sales-heavy wording,
- keep CTA styling consistent across pages,
- closing CTAs should feel confident, not pushy.

---

## Proof and Credibility Rules

Proof should appear throughout the site in disciplined ways.

### Good proof formats
- short credibility strips,
- concise metrics,
- experience framing,
- selected examples,
- structured authority statements.

### Avoid
- overloading the page with numbers,
- placing all proof at the bottom,
- making pages feel like a résumé or pitch deck.

### Rule
Proof should support the narrative early enough to matter.

---

## Navigation and Footer Rules

### Header
- compact
- clear
- consistent
- not oversized

### Footer
- clean
- secondary
- not content-heavy
- consistent across pages

Footer should include:
- brand
- core navigation
- email
- LinkedIn
- copyright

Do not turn the footer into another marketing section.

---

## Consistency Rules Across Pages

All pages should feel like part of the same system.

### Maintain consistency in
- typography scale,
- section spacing,
- button styles,
- card behavior,
- heading tone,
- content density,
- layout rhythm,
- visual restraint.

### Do not allow
- one page to feel oversized and dramatic,
- another to feel compressed and plain,
- service pages to feel unrelated to homepage style,
- content pages to drift into a separate visual language.

---

## What to Preserve From the Existing Brand Reference

Preserve:
- editorial seriousness,
- premium restraint,
- strong white space discipline,
- minimal color use,
- professional tone.

Do not preserve:
- oversized hero scale,
- weak page order,
- flat capabilities listing,
- delayed proof placement,
- content sprawl on important pages.

---

## Universal Implementation Priorities

When designing or refining any page, prioritize in this order:

1. information hierarchy,
2. typography control,
3. section clarity,
4. consistency with site-wide style,
5. proof placement,
6. CTA logic,
7. polish.

Do not begin with decorative tweaks.
Do not solve structure problems with styling alone.

---

## Page Review Checklist

Before finalizing a page, confirm:

- Does the page have a clear purpose?
- Is the opening section tight and readable?
- Is the typography appropriately controlled?
- Are sections ordered logically?
- Is the content specific rather than generic?
- Is proof used well?
- Are CTAs clear and limited?
- Does the page feel consistent with the homepage and the rest of the site?
- Does the page feel premium, restrained, and credible?

If not, revise.

---

## Final Rule

Every page should look like it belongs to the same firm, the same design system, and the same editorial standard.

The site is not a collection of independent pages.
It is one professional platform with one visual language and one level of quality.
