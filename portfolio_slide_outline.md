# Portfolio Slide Outline (NDA-Safe, 9 Slides)

## Slide 1 - Why This Portfolio Exists
- Key message: This is a work sample, not a product pitch or a resume recap.
- Suggested visual: cover page with the lifecycle reimbursement architecture as a background motif.
- Speaker notes: State that the portfolio shows how Joseph translates reimbursement mechanics into commercially usable strategy across the product lifecycle and across the full market access surface area.

## Slide 2 - Why Joseph Fits Complex Provider-Administered Products
- Key message: Joseph combines manufacturer reimbursement, biosimilars commercialization, provider economics, market access analytics, and policy translation.
- Suggested visual: proof-point strip with four tiles: launch planning, in-market management, payer/provider economics, and policy translation.
- Speaker notes: Emphasize that the fit is not generic market access; it is specifically around assets where payment mechanics drive uptake and where leaders need breadth, not just launch support.

## Slide 3 - Reimbursement Architecture Across Sites of Care
- Key message: IPPS, NTAP, HOPPS/OPPS, PFS, Medicaid, MA, MA-PD, commercial, and 340B create different adoption incentives.
- Suggested visual: `visuals/reimbursement_pathway_map.png`
- Speaker notes: Walk the audience through how the same product can behave very differently by setting and channel.

## Slide 4 - Launch Economics Pressure Map
- Key message: Launch volume can rise while realized net falls.
- Suggested visual: `visuals/launch_economics_pressure_map.png`
- Speaker notes: Explain the compounding effects of reimbursement lag, hospital absorption, 340B concentration, contracting pressure, and channel leakage.

## Slide 5 - On-Market / Mid-Cycle Landscape Assessment
- Key message: The right benchmark for a mature product is not launch behavior; it is what stable analogs and current channel mix actually show.
- Suggested visual: `visuals/on_market_midcycle_landscape_assessment.png`
- Speaker notes: Show how reimbursement maturity, formulary/Part D control, site-of-care sensitivity, GTN exposure, and channel behavior change once the product is past launch.

## Slide 6 - Structural vs Recoverable Losses
- Key message: Strategy improves when the team separates unavoidable economics from recoverable leakage.
- Suggested visual: `visuals/recoverable_vs_structural_losses.png`
- Speaker notes: Use this slide to sort policy baseline exposure, execution failure, and strategic recovery levers.

## Slide 7 - What Manufacturers Usually Miss
- Key message: The common failure is treating clinical strength as if it can override reimbursement friction and lifecycle differences.
- Suggested visual: a "blind spots" grid covering launch, in-market, coding, timing, site mix, 340B, Part D, and GTN.
- Speaker notes: Call out that the real problem is usually not lack of demand; it is misread economics, misread mix, or using the wrong benchmark.

## Slide 8 - Joseph's Operating Method
- Key message: The process is assess, quantify, segment, stress-test, recover, operationalize.
- Suggested visual: `visuals/joseph_approach_framework.png`
- Speaker notes: Make clear that the deliverable is a decision system, not a one-time memo.

## Slide 9 - First 90 Days on a New Asset
- Key message: Early work should reduce avoidable launch-year value loss and establish an in-market operating model.
- Suggested visual: phased roadmap or account workplan.
- Speaker notes:
  - 0-30: pathway map, GTN/leakage baseline, segment definition.
  - 31-60: scenario stress-test, coding and contracting actions, evidence gap review.
  - 61-90: field tools, KPI governance, account prioritization, and refresh loop.
  - Tie each workstream back to launch, in-market, and head-of-market-access decision needs.
