## Executive Summary

Not every sponsor carries the same access risk profile. In the latest SPUF window, sponsor activity clusters combine with low-cost generic tier pressure to produce a practical watchlist for account teams.

This is a behavioral signal, not a direct margin measurement.

## Key Takeaways

1. Sponsor behavior is materially heterogeneous.
2. High churn alone is not enough, overlap with tier pressure matters.
3. Cluster-based watchlists are more practical than broad league tables.
4. Outlier handling should be explicit before publication.

## Main Insight

A disciplined sponsor watchlist can be built by layering:
- repricing intensity,
- churn intensity,
- low-cost generic tier-worsening overlap.

This approach is more commercially useful than a single ranking and aligns better with payer-facing operating decisions.

## Practical implication

Use the watchlist for prioritized sponsor engagement, internal escalation, and evidence-pack sequencing. Keep all public claims bounded to observed behavior and inferential interpretation.
