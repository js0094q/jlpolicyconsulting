## Executive Summary

The core signal in the latest SPUF comparison window is not just unit-cost movement. It is the combination of high sponsor activity, large panel churn, and tier worsening in selected low-cost generic concepts. Taken together, these patterns are consistent with sponsors using tier placement as an economics lever when direct pricing interpretation is noisy.

That interpretation should be treated as an inference. The data supports behavior that is consistent with economics protection, but does not directly prove realized margin changes.

## Research Question

Are sponsors moving low-cost generics to higher tiers in a way that is consistent with offsetting economics pressure, after controlling for same-NDC pricing and panel churn?

## Lead Evidence

### Pair-level decomposition
- `2025Q3_to_2025Q4`: matched share `94.48%`, add/drop share `5.52%`
- `2025Q4_to_2026Q1`: matched share `59.81%`, add/drop share `40.19%`

The second window has much heavier composition shift, which means headline delta statistics can conceal mechanism.

### Generic tier movement under same-NDC controls
Top low-cost generic concepts continue to show net higher-tier movement under same-NDC controls in the local analysis outputs.

This matters because it reduces a common false narrative, that every access shift is simply a price-shift signal.

### Sponsor-level heterogeneity
Local sponsor clustering shows four distinct behavioral profiles:
- repricing_dominant
- churn_dominant
- mixed_high_activity
- mixed_moderate

This supports a targeted strategy lens rather than a single market-average interpretation.

## Main Analysis

### 1) Why decomposition is required
The two quarter-pair windows are not behaviorally equivalent. A high-continuity window and a high-churn window should not be interpreted with the same narrative template.

### 2) Why cheap-generic tier movement is commercially meaningful
If low-cost generics move to less favorable tiers while same-NDC prices remain stable, the strategic effect is still real for access teams. Field strategy, payer engagement, and affordability messaging all absorb that change.

### 3) Why sponsor clusters matter
A sponsor with high churn and high repricing behaves differently from one with strong repricing and low churn. These differences matter for account prioritization, contracting posture, and escalation logic.

## Practical Implications

1. Replace single headline price narratives with decomposition-first reporting.
2. Add low-cost-generic tier pressure as a standing signal in sponsor surveillance.
3. Prioritize sponsor watchlists where tier pressure and activity overlap.
4. Keep all client-facing language explicit on observed versus inferred claims.

## Methodology Note

- Matching grain uses plan and product keys with days-supply controls.
- Generic tier signal uses same-NDC controls to limit false repricing attribution.
- Sponsor segmentation uses behavioral buckets from published scorecard outputs.
- Interpretation is descriptive and inferential, not causal.

## Conclusion

The evidence is strongest when presented as a combined mechanism story: high sponsor activity plus low-cost generic tier migration in a high-churn environment. That pattern is consistent with economics defense behavior and is commercially actionable, provided the margin claim remains explicitly inferential.
