## Executive Summary

A growing share of the commercial signal in Part D appears to be in access mechanics, not just direct pricing movement. In selected low-cost generic concepts, tier placement worsens while same-NDC pricing stays comparatively stable. This supports a margin-defense interpretation at the plan-design layer.

The interpretation remains inferential. The analysis identifies behavior consistent with economics protection, but does not directly measure realized sponsor margin.

## Research Thesis

Low-cost generic tier deterioration in the latest SPUF window is better explained as access design pressure under economics constraints than as straightforward same-NDC repricing.

## Key Findings

1. Tier movement and same-NDC price movement are not consistently aligned.
2. Net higher-tier share increases are visible in selected low-cost concepts.
3. Same-NDC controls materially improve claim quality.
4. Mix-shift effects remain an explicit caveat.

## Why This Matters

Manufacturers, payer strategy teams, and access functions are exposed to these tier mechanics regardless of whether same-NDC prices appear calm. Tier assignment still affects practical access conditions, operational burden, and commercial planning.

## Analytical guardrails

- Report observed movement separately from inferred motive.
- Use same-NDC controls as a default requirement.
- Avoid sweeping market-wide motive claims from sparse concept evidence.
