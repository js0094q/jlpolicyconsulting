## Operational meaning for a Part B reimbursement director

**Confirmed fact:** CMS’s April 2026 ASP Pricing Files page says Medicare generally publishes Part B payment limits quarterly and that claims can still be processed locally when a payment limit is not listed. CMS guidance on IPAY 2028 and MFP effectuation shows that negotiated-price implementation has to be coordinated with the existing ASP/payment-limit framework. [ASP Pricing Files](https://www.cms.gov/medicare/payment/part-b-drugs/asp-pricing-files), [IPAY 2028 final guidance](https://www.cms.gov/files/document/ipay-2028-final-guidance.pdf)

**Commercial interpretation:** once a Part B AbbVie product is selected, the issue is not only the negotiated price itself. The issue is how that price interacts with ASP reporting, quarterly payment-limit files, provider reimbursement, charge capture, waste management, and field messaging. That is exactly why a Director of Government Affairs - U.S. Reimbursement role is operationally different from a pure policy role.

**Implication for Botox:** the brand is multi-indication, high-volume, and supplied in single-dose vials. The public FDA label says BOTOX is supplied in 100 Unit and 200 Unit vials and is single-dose only, which makes discarded-drug compliance, JW/JZ behavior, and site-level waste management especially relevant. CMS’s discarded-drug policy says JZ is required when there are no discarded amounts from single-dose containers or single-use packages, and JW/JZ compliance affects claim correctness. [BOTOX label](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2023/103000s5327lbl.pdf), [CMS discarded-drug article](https://www.cms.gov/medicare-coverage-database/view/article.aspx?CptHcpcsCode=j1561&articleId=52855)

**Implication for the role:** a reimbursement director at AbbVie would need a standing model that answers four questions at the same time: what the negotiated price is, what CMS payment-limit files will do to ASP-based reimbursement, how provider economics change under the new price, and how to message the change to physician offices, hospitals, and societies without creating operational confusion.

# 8. Where Part B and Part D Intersect for AbbVie

## 1. Skyrizi is the clearest cross-benefit case
**Confirmed fact:** AbbVie’s 2025 Form 10-K says Skyrizi is given as three induction doses via IV infusion for Crohn’s disease and ulcerative colitis, followed by SC maintenance. The FDA label also shows both IV and SC presentations. [AbbVie 2025 Form 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1551152/000155115226000008/abbv-20251231.htm), [SKYRIZI label](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2024/761105s032lbl.pdf)

**Commercial interpretation:** one brand must be managed across an infusion-center buy-and-bill pathway and a specialty-pharmacy / self-administered pathway. That means CMS coding, provider education, and plan formularies all matter at once.

**Why this role cares:** the person owns the translation between benefit design and launch/lifecycle execution. A dual-pathway brand like Skyrizi is exactly where that translation fails if nobody owns it.

## 2. Oncology regimen economics do not stop at the molecule
**Confirmed fact:** AbbVie’s 2025 Form 10-K shows Venclexta, Imbruvica, and Elahere all as meaningful oncology brands, with Venclexta and Imbruvica oral and Elahere infused. [AbbVie 2025 Form 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1551152/000155115226000008/abbv-20251231.htm)

**Commercial interpretation:** a Part D oral agent can still be part of a clinic-based regimen, and a Part B infused agent can influence the economics of the total regimen. Reimbursement strategy therefore has to be regimen-aware, not molecule-only.

**Why this role cares:** the director must anticipate how a Part D decision changes clinic economics and how a Part B decision changes therapy sequencing, support, and site-of-care choices.

## 3. Botox shows how Part B negotiation spills into provider operations
**Confirmed fact:** CMS selected Botox/Botox Cosmetic for IPAY 2028, and AbbVie’s 2025 Form 10-K confirms the selection. [CMS 2028 selected-drug list](https://www.cms.gov/files/document/factsheet-medicare-negotiation-selected-drug-list-ipay-2028.pdf), [AbbVie 2025 Form 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1551152/000155115226000008/abbv-20251231.htm)

**Commercial interpretation:** Botox is multi-indication, billed by HCPCS unit, and supplied in single-dose vials. That makes negotiation, ASP, waste, and JZ/JW compliance part of one operational file. It is not a policy abstraction.

## 4. Part D negotiation lessons create enterprise doctrine
**Confirmed fact:** AbbVie already has 2026 and 2027 negotiated prices for Imbruvica, Vraylar, and Linzess. [CMS 2026 negotiated prices](https://www.cms.gov/files/document/fact-sheet-negotiated-prices-initial-price-applicability-year-2026.pdf), [CMS 2027 negotiated prices](https://www.cms.gov/files/document/fact-sheet-negotiated-prices-ipay-2027.pdf)

**Commercial interpretation:** the company can now compare one completed cycle, one active cycle, and one future cycle. That gives AbbVie enough data to build a real pricing-playbook doctrine for future Part B and Part D negotiations.

## D. IRA answer
**Prompt:** What does AbbVie need to be thinking about as Medicare negotiation expands into Part B?

AbbVie needs to think about negotiation as an operational reimbursement event, not only a price event. With Part B in the program, the price flows into ASP-based payment-limit logic, discarded-drug behavior, site-of-care economics, and provider messaging. That is different from a Part D-only negotiation, where the immediate operational issues are mostly formulary and dispensing. For AbbVie, the lesson from Imbruvica, Vraylar, and Linzess is that the company should already have a doctrine for how to respond when CMS selects a product. For Botox specifically, the key is to be ready before the MFP is public because providers will need a clean explanation of how the policy affects coding, payment, and waste. The strongest companies here will not just react to CMS. They will enter the cycle with a reimbursement playbook that can absorb the policy change without losing provider confidence.
