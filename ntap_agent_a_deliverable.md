## NTAP Application Structure

The NTAP application should be framed as a controlled reimbursement dossier, not as a clinical white paper. The applicant should open with the technology and indication, identify the FDA status and timing, show how the technology will be coded on the inpatient claim, then connect that coding to the relevant MS-DRGs. From there, the application needs to prove newness, quantify the cost gap against the DRG payment, and then show substantial clinical improvement with evidence that CMS can review and, where allowed, publicly summarize.

For a client-facing draft, the most useful structure is: applicant/contact, technology description and inpatient use, FDA pathway and timing, ICD-10-PCS and MS-DRG mapping, newness narrative, cost and volume narrative, cost-criterion workbook support, and SCI evidence. That sequence tracks the MEARIS logic and avoids a common sponsor mistake: starting with outcomes before the claim mechanics are established.

Inference: The product-specific NTAP payment convention is best modeled as the lesser of the NTAP percentage of technology cost or the excess of estimated costs over the applicable DRG payment. Use that as a working assumption unless and until you confirm the exact percentage and cap in the relevant final rule.

Inference: The central economic point is not that NTAP makes Product X profitable on day one. It narrows the gap long enough for adoption to occur while CMS and claims data work toward DRG recalibration.

## Practical Sponsor Failure Points

Fact: CMS explicitly requires coding specificity, cost support, volume estimates, and a claim-based rationale for why the technology is new and clinically improved. [FY2027_NTAP_Application_Overview--Traditional.pdf](/Users/josephstewart/Downloads/fy2027_ntap_application-resources-for-reference-only/FY2027_NTAP_Application_Overview--Traditional.pdf)

Inference: The most common sponsor failures are weak claim coding, unsupported volume estimates, a cost model that does not isolate incremental technology cost, failure to use the workbook structure correctly, and an application that treats the indication as a single generic use case instead of a separate reimbursement story.

Inference: Another recurring failure point is over-indexing on clinical outcomes without showing how those outcomes translate into inpatient economics. CMS can credit SCI, but the add-on payment still turns on the coded claim and the cost criterion.
