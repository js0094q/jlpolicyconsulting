# Joseph Stewart | Market Access Portfolio

## Portfolio Overview
This portfolio is designed to show the work itself, how the work is structured, what questions it answers, and why the resulting outputs are useful. It is not a resume in brochure form, and it does not rely on product-specific detail. The visuals carry the primary narrative. The copy is there to clarify the business meaning, the analytical logic, and the utility of each output.

The through-line across the portfolio is consistent: complex products do not succeed on clinical value alone. They succeed or stall based on how reimbursement, formulary design, site-of-care economics, provider incentives, channel mix, and policy structure interact in the real market.

## Core Story
The portfolio is built around a simple idea: reimbursement mechanics are only useful if they are translated into decisions.

The work shown here is designed to make complex access and reimbursement environments legible across launch, in-market, and mature lifecycle stages. It is meant to clarify where economics are supportive, where they are fragile, where access is nominal versus practical, and where observed performance is being driven by real demand versus reimbursement or channel distortion.

Several principles anchor the work throughout:

- Clinical value alone does not determine uptake.
- Reimbursement architecture changes adoption behavior.
- Formulary position is not the same as practical access.
- 340B is an economics variable, not just a policy or compliance variable.
- Gross-to-net pressure has to be decomposed before it can be improved.
- The most useful work is site-specific, benefit-specific, and operationally actionable.
- The output has to be clear enough to guide real decisions, not just describe complexity.

## What the Work Covers
The work spans the major access and reimbursement environments that shape product performance.

It includes:
- Medicaid and state-level access variability.
- Medicare Part B economics, including provider spread and reimbursement timing.
- Inpatient hospital reimbursement and bundled-payment pressure.
- Hospital outpatient reimbursement and site-of-care implications.
- Physician-office economics and buy-and-bill sensitivity.
- Part D formulary design, tiering, utilization management, and patient affordability.
- MA-PD crossover dynamics, where medical-benefit and pharmacy-benefit incentives intersect.
- 340B-sensitive channel behavior and mix distortion.
- On-market and mid-cycle landscape assessment.
- Gross-to-net pressure mapping and recovery logic.
- Visual frameworks and operating tools that turn analysis into usable strategy.

## What the Portfolio Is Showing
This portfolio is intended to show a pattern of work.

That pattern includes:
- mapping how products behave differently across reimbursement systems,
- benchmarking launch and in-market economics against realistic market conditions,
- identifying where access is constrained by design rather than by product value,
- separating structural losses from controllable leakage,
- and converting those findings into clear visual tools and practical decision frameworks.

The emphasis is not on describing every rule. It is on showing how those rules change market behavior and how the resulting insights can be organized into visuals that are immediately useful.

## Visual Narrative

### Reimbursement pathway map
This visual shows how the same product can behave differently depending on whether it sits in Medicaid, Part B, inpatient hospital, hospital outpatient, physician office, Part D, or an integrated MA-PD structure.

The purpose of the visual is to prevent blended thinking. It separates payment logic, adoption incentives, and operational realities by setting so the audience can see where economics diverge and why a single national story is usually misleading.

### Launch economics pressure map
This visual shows where launch performance can look healthy on the surface while underlying economics deteriorate.

It is designed to make pressure visible across reimbursement lag, provider margin, site-of-care variation, contracting burden, 340B concentration, and channel mix. The point is to distinguish early volume from healthy economics and to show where pressure is structural versus where it may be recoverable.

### On-market / mid-cycle landscape assessment
This visual is not a launch view. It is a benchmark view.

It shows how products behave once reimbursement pathways are established, contracts have refreshed, channel patterns have settled, and formulary, 340B, or utilization-management dynamics are more visible. Its purpose is to show what normal market behavior looks like once the launch period has passed and to provide a benchmark against which other products or strategies can be interpreted.

### Recoverable versus structural losses
This visual separates unavoidable economics from controllable leakage.

It is designed to clarify which pressures are inherent to the market and which are more likely to improve through better reimbursement design, channel architecture, evidence framing, account prioritization, or operational execution. The utility of the visual is that it moves the conversation from barrier-listing to decision-making.

### End-to-end approach framework
This visual shows how the work is approached from assessment through execution.

It lays out a repeatable logic:
- assess the environment,
- quantify the economics,
- segment the pressure points,
- stress-test the scenarios,
- identify recovery levers,
- and convert the findings into usable tools.

Its value is that it shows the work as a system, not as a collection of isolated reimbursement observations.

## What Makes the Work Useful
The value of this work is not simply that it explains access complexity. The value is that it organizes that complexity into formats that are usable.

The outputs are designed to help clarify:
- where a product is economically viable,
- where it is exposed,
- where practical access differs from formal coverage,
- where site-of-care assumptions are masking risk,
- where formulary or tiering changes are shifting behavior,
- where integrated plan design changes incentives,
- where 340B is distorting mix,
- and where the organization is dealing with structural economics versus execution issues.

The portfolio is meant to show that the work produces not just analysis, but clarity.
