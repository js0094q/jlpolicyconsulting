## NTAP Appendix

### NTAP Application Structure

`Fact:` CMS evaluates NTAP eligibility based on three statutory criteria: newness, cost inadequacy relative to the MS-DRG payment, and substantial clinical improvement. The FY 2027 traditional-pathway application is structured around those three tests and requires the applicant to build a coherent record across contact information, technology description, FDA status, coding, cost, volume, newness, and SCI. Source: [/Users/josephstewart/Downloads/fy2027_ntap_application-resources-for-reference-only/FY2027_NTAP_Application_Overview--Traditional.pdf](/Users/josephstewart/Downloads/fy2027_ntap_application-resources-for-reference-only/FY2027_NTAP_Application_Overview--Traditional.pdf)

For client purposes, the application should be understood as a reimbursement dossier rather than a clinical monograph. The opening sections establish the applicant, the technology, and the inpatient use case. The next sections establish the FDA pathway and timing, including whether approval has already been received or whether the marketing authorization request has been filed or accepted and remains active. The coding section then connects the technology to the inpatient claim by identifying the relevant ICD-10-CM diagnosis codes, ICD-10-PCS procedure codes, and MS-DRGs. Only after those claim mechanics are clear should the application turn to the statutory criteria: newness, cost, and substantial clinical improvement.

The most durable client-ready sequence is: applicant and contacts; technology description and inpatient use; FDA pathway and timing; coding and MS-DRG mapping; newness demonstration; cost and volume; cost criterion; and substantial clinical improvement. That sequence matches how CMS evaluates the file in MEARIS and avoids a common sponsor error, which is to lead with outcomes messaging before establishing how the technology is actually identified and paid on the inpatient claim.

`Fact:` CMS requires a separate application for each distinct technology or service and recommends a separate application for each indication because newness and SCI are assessed separately by indication. Source: [/Users/josephstewart/Downloads/fy2027_ntap_application-resources-for-reference-only/FY2027_NTAP_Application_Overview--Traditional.pdf](/Users/josephstewart/Downloads/fy2027_ntap_application-resources-for-reference-only/FY2027_NTAP_Application_Overview--Traditional.pdf)

`Fact:` At minimum, the application package must support FDA status, coding, cost, clinical evidence, and volume. In practice, that means the sponsor needs: FDA approval or an active filed-and-accepted submission; an ICD-10-PCS strategy; ICD-10-CM and MS-DRG mapping; the required NTAP cost analysis spreadsheet and related codes/MS-DRGs workbook; SCI evidence tied to one or more CMS sub-criteria; and inpatient volume projections for Medicare and non-Medicare populations. `Fact:` CMS accepts FY 2027 NTAP applications only through MEARIS. The overview states that the document is only an overview and does not replace the application in MEARIS, that MEARIS is only accessible from within the United States, and that all fields are required unless marked optional. The relevant sponsor task is to translate those product assumptions into a CMS-style cost story. CMS is not asking whether the technology is clinically attractive in the abstract. CMS is asking whether, once the claim is identified and standardized, the episode economics still sit above the DRG payment envelope.

#### Scenario 1: No NTAP

`Inference:` Illustrative base case:

| Metric | Value |
|---|---:|
| DRG payment | $30,000 |
| Product X cost | $25,000 |
| Other costs | $20,000 |
| Total cost | $45,000 |
| Margin | -$15,000 |

Without NTAP, the hospital absorbs the full incremental technology cost inside a fixed inpatient payment. On this logic, Product X is economically unattractive at scale even if the therapy is clinically compelling. Any adoption that occurs is likely to be selective and sponsor-supported rather than broad and routine.

#### Scenario 2: NTAP Applied

`Inference:` Illustrative bridge case, assuming NTAP pays the lesser of the applicable NTAP percentage of the technology cost or the amount by which estimated costs exceed DRG payment. Because the supplied CMS materials do not state the current NTAP percentage, the 65 percent assumption should be validated against the applicable final rule before submission.

| Metric | Value |
|---|---:|
| Product cost | $25,000 |
| Modeled NTAP at 65% | $16,250 |
| Net hospital product exposure | $8,750 |

Under this bridge scenario, hospital exposure improves materially but does not necessarily disappear. The case remains financially sensitive, but the technology can move from clearly loss-making to potentially manageable in selected patients and higher-acuity centers.

#### Scenario 3: Post-NTAP / DRG Reweighting

`Inference:` Illustrative end-state case:

| Metric | Value |
|---|---:|
| DRG payment | $50,000 |
| Total cost | $45,000 |
| Margin | $5,000 |

This is the strategic end state. Sustainable adoption does not come from temporary add-on support alone. It comes when the underlying DRG better reflects the resource use associated with the technology.

The key analytical insight is straightforward: clinical value alone does not drive adoption. The binding constraint is whether incremental technology cost exceeds the embedded DRG margin. NTAP can partially bridge that gap, but durable adoption requires payment recalibration at the DRG level.

## Coding Clarification Insert

### Coding Reality in Inpatient Setting

`Fact:` CMS frames the NTAP application around inpatient claim identification. The application asks the sponsor to identify the ICD-10-CM diagnosis codes tied to the indication, the ICD-10-PCS procedure codes used to identify the technology, and the MS-DRGs to which the cases map. CMS also states that, for add-on payment to occur, the technology must be distinctly identifiable on the inpatient claim by a unique code such as an ICD-10-PCS code, with or without ICD-10-CM diagnosis support. Source: [/Users/josephstewart/Downloads/fy2027_ntap_application-resources-for-reference-only/FY2027_NTAP_Application_Overview--Traditional.pdf](/Users/josephstewart/Downloads/fy2027_ntap_application-resources-for-reference-only/FY2027_NTAP_Application_Overview--Traditional.pdf)

`Inference:` In practical IPPS terms, inpatient reimbursement is driven by MS-DRG assignment, not by HCPCS or J-codes. Drugs and biologicals are generally packaged inside the DRG unless an exception applies, most notably NTAP or outlier payment.

### What Coding Applies

`Fact:` For NTAP purposes, the operative coding set is ICD-10-CM plus ICD-10-PCS tied to the relevant MS-DRGs. CMS requires the applicant to explain whether current ICD-10-PCS codes uniquely identify the technology, whether a unique code request has been made or will be made, and which MS-DRGs the technology currently maps to. CMS separately requires the sponsor to complete a codes/MS-DRGs workbook showing how cases were selected for the cost analysis. Source: [/Users/josephstewart/Downloads/fy2027_ntap_application-resources-for-reference-only/FY2027_NTAP_Application_Overview--Traditional.pdf](/Users/josephstewart/Downloads/fy2027_ntap_application-resources-for-reference-only/FY2027_NTAP_Application_Overview--Traditional.pdf), [/Users/josephstewart/Downloads/fy2027_ntap_application-resources-for-reference-only/FY2027_NTAP_Cost_Criterion_Codes_and_MS-DRGs.xlsx](/Users/josephstewart/Downloads/fy2027_ntap_application-resources-for-reference-only/FY2027_NTAP_Cost_Criterion_Codes_and_MS-DRGs.xlsx)

`Inference:` J-codes are relevant in outpatient or Part B billing contexts, but they are not what makes a technology visible for inpatient NTAP purposes. Likewise, the X-modifiers `XE`, `XP`, `XS`, and `XU` are NCCI bypass modifiers used in outpatient or professional billing. They do not affect MS-DRG assignment, NTAP eligibility, or inpatient payment visibility.

### What Actually Matters

The only coding element that determines NTAP eligibility and payment visibility in IPPS is the presence of a distinct ICD-10-PCS procedure code that reliably identifies use of the technology on the inpatient claim. Diagnosis coding and DRG mapping still matter because they define the clinical population and the payment groups used in the cost analysis, but they do not substitute for a claim-level identifier for the technology itself.

The sponsor's operational burden, therefore, is not to show that a J-code exists or that modifier logic is tidy. It is to demonstrate that Product X can be distinctly identified on the inpatient claim, that the resulting cases can be assigned to the correct MS-DRGs, and that the cost model uses those coded claims to show payment inadequacy under the existing DRG structure.

### NTAP Timeline (Step-by-Step)

`Fact:` For FY 2027, CMS's operative calendar begins with application submission through MEARIS by October 6, 2025. Supplemental information that the sponsor wants guaranteed for inclusion in the proposed rule must be submitted by December 15, 2025. CMS states that the annual town hall is expected in December 2025, and traditional-pathway applicants generally present there on substantial clinical improvement. FDA approval or clearance must be in hand by May 1, 2026. ### DRG Evolution in Stroke and EVT

`Fact:` The CMS cost workbook is explicitly constructed around historical claims logic, case weighting, and prior-year thresholds. It states that applicants typically use the most recent available full-year MedPAR file, and the FY 2027 application materials illustrate that FY 2024 claims are the most recent typical source year for the upcoming cycle. `Inference:` DRGs are backward-looking because they are recalibrated from historical claims data rather than prospective sponsor narratives. For stroke and EVT, that means the current DRG assignment does not immediately move just because Product X enters the market. The new cost only affects the payment system after sufficient claim experience enters the recalibration process.

`Fact:` The user's strategic framing uses MS-DRGs 023 and 024 for EVT and 061 through 063 for non-EVT stroke. The CMS workbook confirms that those DRGs exist in the threshold framework used for NTAP cost analysis, but the workbook values are thresholds rather than payment rates. `Inference:` A practical planning assumption is that Product X launch occurs in a reimbursement lag period. In other words, launch is the cost-center period, NTAP is the transition period, and DRG recalibration is the long-term payment end state.

### Strategic Implications for Product X

The strategic implication is that Product X should not be commercialized as though clinical value automatically produces hospital adoption. The more accurate reimbursement narrative is operational: launch creates financial friction, NTAP narrows that friction, and only later DRG change creates a stable economic landing zone. If the sponsor prices as though the end-state DRG already exists, adoption risk rises materially. If the sponsor instead uses NTAP as the bridge and builds the coding and evidence package needed for later DRG recalibration, the reimbursement story becomes internally coherent.

## Practical Considerations

`Inference:` Sponsors most often fail in four places. First, the cost model is weak because it does not isolate incremental technology cost from the rest of the stay or does not explain removed and added charges cleanly. Second, the DRG mapping is imprecise, which undermines both the cost analysis and the inpatient use narrative. Third, the application does not show how standardized and inflated charges exceed the applicable threshold under the CMS workbook logic. Fourth, the coding story is underdeveloped because the technology is not uniquely identifiable by ICD-10-PCS or the sponsor has not aligned the NTAP application with the ICD-10 C&M pathway.

`Fact:` CMS's own materials reinforce those failure points by requiring coding specificity, a separate codes/MS-DRGs workbook, source-year justification, supporting data, preserved formulas, and step-by-step explanation of the cost analysis columns.
