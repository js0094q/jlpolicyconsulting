# Research Appendix | Reimbursement and Access Framework (NDA-Safe)

**Prepared:** April 8, 2026 (ET)

## A. IPPS / NTAP
- CMS states that NTAP is a transitional add-on payment intended to help accommodate the period before a new technology's costs are reflected in MS-DRG weights.[O1][O2][O3]
- The current CMS process runs through MEARIS and uses annual cycle materials, including application guidance, public resources, and timing windows.[O1][O15]
- The NTAP framework is anchored to three questions: is the technology new, is the standard payment inadequate in the relevant DRG, and does the technology show substantial clinical improvement?[O1][O2]
- NTAP is not a permanent fix. It is a bridge that reduces early loss but still leaves hospitals with residual margin, workflow, and operational burden.[O2][O3]

**Strategy inference:** Early adoption may improve when NTAP is available, but hospitals still evaluate whether the residual economics and workflow burden are acceptable.

## B. HOPPS / OPPS
- CMS maintains an outpatient payment system that can provide transitional pass-through payment for drugs and biologics that qualify under the outpatient rules.[O4][O5][O6][O7]
- The pass-through window is temporary and the product eventually returns to the standard OPPS payment framework once transitional status ends.[O4][O5][O6]
- Claims visibility matters because payment history and coding feed future rate-setting and packaging logic.[O4][O7]

**Strategy inference:** Launch forecasts should include both transition-period economics and the post-transition reset.

## C. PFS / Physician Office
- CMS' physician-fee framework is distinct from the hospital outpatient and inpatient systems.[O8]
- Part B drugs are generally reimbursed using ASP-based methodology, and CMS updates payment from quarterly manufacturer data submissions.[O9][O10]
- That creates timing mismatch and spread risk in buy-and-bill settings when acquisition cost, payment timing, and patient mix do not align cleanly.[O9][O10]

**Strategy inference:** Office economics should never be assumed from HOPD economics.

## D. Medicaid, MA, MA-PD, and Commercial
- Medicaid is not a single national commercial behavior pattern; state FFS and managed care rules can change rebate exposure, utilization controls, and realized net.
- Medicare Advantage and MA-PD repackage reimbursement through plan-level cost sharing, prior authorization, formulary rules, network design, and benefit architecture.
- Commercial plans can move faster or slower than Medicare on utilization management, site-of-care controls, and channel restrictions.

**Strategy inference:** The access story needs to be segmented by benefit design and channel, not rolled into one payer average.

## E. Part D / Formulary
- CMS maintains Part D formulary data files that include cost-share tier level, step therapy, prior authorization, and quantity-limit indicators for Medicare Prescription Drug Plans and MA-PD plans.[O16]
- CMS guidance also recognizes formulary exceptions and utilization-management requirements such as prior authorization, step therapy, and quantity limits as part of Part D access management.[O17]
- For mid-cycle products, the Part D view matters because access can shift when the commercial story moves from buy-and-bill exposure to pharmacy-benefit coverage, tiering, and exception workflows.

**Strategy inference:** A realistic mid-cycle assessment needs both a medical-benefit lens and a Part D/formulary lens; otherwise it misses a large part of the access equation.

## F. 340B
- HRSA defines covered-entity eligibility and registration through the 340B program.[O11][O12]
- HRSA's FAQ confirms that 340B pricing applies to covered outpatient drugs.[O13]
- CMS outpatient policy materials explicitly address 340B-related outpatient payment mechanics.[O14]
- The 340B Medicaid exclusion framework is also part of launch planning because duplicate-discount management and the Medicaid Exclusion File can matter operationally.[O15]

**Strategy inference:** 340B can improve site-level willingness to use a product while simultaneously worsening realized net if early volume concentrates in covered entities.

## G. Product-Relevant Clinical and Economic Context
- The inpatient launch materials show the kinds of questions that matter in this category: post-procedure cost, ICU utilization, infusion burden, DRG offset, and whether NTAP changes adoption risk.
- The response draft frames the launch as a reimbursement-sequenced inpatient problem: coding visibility, transitional payment support, and permanent DRG alignment have to work together.
- Directionally, high-acuity pathways often involve inpatient and ICU resource use, and prolonged infusion can matter operationally as well as economically.

**Strategy inference:** Operational burden is both an adoption barrier and a reimbursement signal when the product is used in a hospital pathway.

## H. Key Definitions
- **IPPS:** Inpatient case-based payment architecture centered on MS-DRG.
- **NTAP:** Transitional add-on payment for qualifying inpatient technologies.
- **OPPS / HOPPS:** Hospital outpatient payment architecture with transitional pathways for certain drugs and biologics.
- **PFS:** Physician fee framework, often paired with ASP-based Part B drug reimbursement in office pathways.
- **340B:** Statutory outpatient drug discount framework for eligible covered entities.
- **Part D:** Prescription drug benefit administered by Medicare plans, including formularies, tiering, prior authorization, step therapy, and exceptions.
- **MA:** Medicare Advantage, where plan-level utilization management affects access.
- **MA-PD:** Medicare Advantage Part D, where pharmacy-benefit design can create separate access friction.

## I. Known vs Inferred vs Client Data Needed

### Known (source-grounded)
- NTAP criteria, timing structure, and MEARIS workflow.
- OPPS pass-through mechanics and their temporary nature.
- ASP timing mechanics for Part B drugs.
- 340B eligibility and outpatient scope.

### Inferred (strategy judgment)
- Magnitude of residual provider loss under transitional states.
- Size of buy-and-bill spread compression by segment.
- Degree of GTN pressure from 340B concentration.

### Data needed for precise modeling
- Launch price and net architecture.
- Site-of-care mix assumptions and account segmentation.
- Provider adoption governance patterns.
- Reimbursement support operating model and cost assumptions.
- Evidence package details relevant to utilization and economic endpoints.
