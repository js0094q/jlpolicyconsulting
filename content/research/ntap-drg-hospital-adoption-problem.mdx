## Objective

Inpatient launches are often described in clinical terms long before the payment math is settled.
That is usually the wrong order.

For hospitals, the relevant question is not whether a therapy is clinically differentiated.
It is whether the therapy can fit inside the DRG before reimbursement aligns with the cost of use.

## The Launch Constraint

Hospital adoption depends on three linked variables:

1. **Incremental cost**
   The hospital has to absorb the therapy before it can recover value through the claim.
2. **Payment timing**
   NTAP, coding, and DRG recalibration do not happen on the same schedule.
3. **Operational burden**
   Even a manageable therapy can stall if it adds time, coordination, or review friction.

If any one of those pieces is out of sync, launch pressure shifts to the facility instead of the payer.

## Why NTAP Matters

NTAP is not a marketing milestone. It is a bridge.

It narrows the gap between:

- the price the sponsor needs,
- the cost the hospital can actually absorb,
- and the reimbursement structure that will eventually normalize use.

Without that bridge, hospitals often treat a new inpatient therapy as a margin problem first and a clinical opportunity second.

## What the Analysis Should Show

A useful launch review does not stop at the headline DRG.
It should answer:

- What is the incremental cost relative to the bundled case payment?
- How much value can be recovered from shorter LOS, lower downstream utilization, or better discharge disposition?
- Is the gap temporary because of reimbursement timing, or structural because the price is misaligned?

## Commercial Implication

For a hospital product, adoption risk often comes from the reimbursement sequence rather than the evidence package.
That sequence is what determines whether early launch is broad, narrow, or delayed.

## Decision Use

This lens is most useful when teams are deciding:

- whether launch pricing is supportable,
- whether reimbursement timing needs to be part of the launch narrative,
- and whether the evidence package should emphasize economic recovery, not just clinical benefit.

The practical test is simple:

> If reimbursement lags the product, adoption lags the product.
