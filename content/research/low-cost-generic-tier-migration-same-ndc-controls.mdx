## Research question

Can low-cost generic products in Medicare Part D show meaningful access-pressure signals through tier migration even when same-NDC pricing moves are limited, and how should that signal be interpreted across quarter-pair quality contexts?

## Internal evidence set

- `output/exports/spuf-analysis-package/data/repricing_churn_decomposition.csv`
- `output/exports/spuf-analysis-package/data/generic_tier_worsening_top25.csv`
- `output/exports/spuf-analysis-package/data/sponsor_segmentation_summary.csv`
- `output/exports/spuf-analysis-package/data/beneficiary_tier_linkage_delta_q4_to_q1.csv`

## Method

The analysis applies this sequence:

1. Use decomposition of quarter-pair behavior to determine whether the pair is continuity-rich or churn-heavy.
2. Restrict the generic tier test to same-NDC comparable rows to reduce misattribution risk.
3. Evaluate whether directional tier movement aligns with sponsor behavior segmentation and beneficiary-design linkage.

### Quarter-pair decomposition baseline

- `2025Q3 to 2025Q4`
  - matched share: `94.48%`
  - add/drop share: `5.52%`
  - repriced share of matched: `69.73%`
  - unchanged matched share: `30.27%`

- `2025Q4 to 2026Q1`
  - matched share: `59.81%`
  - add/drop share: `40.19%`
  - repriced share of matched: `95.79%`
  - unchanged matched share: `4.21%`

The second window is composition-sensitive, so this study uses it as an alerting context rather than a primary causal layer.

## Key findings

1. **Net higher-tier movement appears in multiple low-cost generic concepts under same-NDC control.**
2. **The decomposed pair structure materially affects inferential confidence.**
3. **Sponsor behavior is heterogeneous, so a single “market answer” is analytically weak.**
4. **Beneficiary-cost linkage is directionally useful but under-specified for exact dollar impact.**

## Inference boundary

- Observed evidence supports a mechanism-consistent signal, not direct causal intent at every sponsor-product row.
- Exact margins, realized sponsor profit impact, and full patient out-of-pocket amounts are not directly derivable from this extraction without deeper utilization and claims-level normalization.

## Practical implication

A stable price-only monitor is insufficient for this setting.

A stronger product-access monitoring model combines:

- matched repricing decomposition,
- same-NDC tier movement,
- sponsor activity class,
- and benefit-friction checks.

This produces cleaner watchlists and reduces false positives driven by cohort composition.
