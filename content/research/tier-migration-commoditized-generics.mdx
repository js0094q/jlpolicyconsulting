The headline finding is simple: tier position can worsen even when same-NDC unit prices barely move.

That matters because access design is not reducible to price alone.

## What was measured

The analysis evaluated mature generic drugs in Medicare Part D across recent quarterly snapshots and compared:

- plan-level tier placement,
- same-NDC unit price change,
- and the direction of movement across plans with valid tier records in both snapshots.

## How to read the result

The key interpretation is narrow: tier position can worsen without meaningful same-NDC price pressure.

That does not prove intent. It does show a recurring pattern consistent with plan optimization behavior that is not reducible to unit price alone.

The most plausible mechanisms are directional, not proven:

- class-level formulary normalization,
- utilization management harmonization,
- channel strategy adjustments,
- plan bid optimization under revised liability conditions.

## Methodology note

<MethodologyNote>
This analysis used CMS Part D Formulary, Pharmacy Network, and Pricing Public Use Files for 2025Q4 and 2026Q1. Drug identity was normalized at the NDC and ingredient level, then tier deltas and same-NDC price changes were compared across products with continuity in both quarters. The result is intended as directional evidence of access design behavior, not causal proof.
</MethodologyNote>

## Bottom line

Tier migration among mature generics appears as a measurable access signal that cannot be explained by same-NDC unit pricing alone.
