## Objective

This analysis evaluates whether mature generic drugs in Medicare Part D experienced net worsening in formulary tier placement between recent quarterly snapshots and whether those shifts aligned with same-NDC unit price changes.

The central question is straightforward: if unit prices remain stable, why does tier position worsen?

## Dataset and Scope

Data sources:

- CMS Part D Formulary, Pharmacy Network, and Pricing Public Use Files (SPUF)
- Plan-level files for 2025Q4 and 2026Q1
- NDC-level pricing fields standardized to same-NDC comparisons across quarters

Scope filters:

- mature generics with broad plan presence
- products with comparable NDC continuity across both quarters
- observations limited to plans with valid tier records in both snapshots

## Methodology

1. Normalize drug identity at the NDC and ingredient level.
2. Calculate plan-level tier deltas for each product.
3. Aggregate weighted and unweighted net higher-tier movement.
4. Calculate same-NDC unit price change over the same period.
5. Flag products with meaningful tier worsening and limited unit price change.

### Metric Definitions

| Metric | Definition |
| --- | --- |
| Net Higher-Tier Movement | Share-point change in plans placing a product in higher non-preferred tiers |
| Same-NDC Unit Price Change | Percent change in mean unit price for matched NDCs across quarters |
| Tier Worsening Event | Product-level movement threshold exceeding pre-set noise band |

## Core Findings

A subset of mature generics showed recurring tier worsening despite limited same-NDC price movement.

| Product | Net Higher-Tier Movement | Same-NDC Unit Price Change |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Nortriptyline HCl | +23.6 pp | +0.7% |
| Alprazolam | +15.2 pp | +1.6% |
| Morphine Sulfate | +7.0 pp | +3.5% |
| Dexmethylphenidate HCl | +6.8 pp | -0.1% |
| Oxycodone HCl | +6.4 pp | +1.5% |

The key interpretation is narrow: **tier position can worsen without meaningful underlying same-NDC price pressure**.

## Interpretation

These files do not prove intent. They do show a recurring pattern consistent with plan optimization behavior that is not reducible to unit price alone.

Mechanisms that are directionally consistent with observed behavior include:

- class-level formulary normalization,
- utilization management harmonization,
- channel strategy adjustments,
- plan bid optimization under revised liability conditions.

## Commercial Implications

For manufacturers and market access teams, the implication is operational.

If analytics only monitor gross price trajectory, access deterioration can be missed until pull-through weakens. Tier movement should be monitored jointly with utilization controls and channel requirements.

## Recommended Monitoring Stack

- Tier Movement Tracker by class and plan segment
- UM Overlay Tracker for PA, ST, and QL changes
- Price vs Access Divergence Monitor at same-NDC resolution

## Limits

- Quarterly snapshots do not capture intra-quarter policy file edits.
- Tier codes require consistent mapping across plan designs.
- Observational files support inference, not intent attribution.

## Bottom Line

Tier migration among mature generics appears as a measurable access signal that cannot be explained by same-NDC unit pricing alone.
