## Objective

This research evaluates how far unit cost data can explain plan behavior in Medicare Part D and where interpretation fails if pricing is analyzed without access design variables.

## Data Inputs

- CMS pricing files with product-level unit cost fields
- Matched formulary and utilization management files
- Plan identifiers enabling segmentation by MA-PD and PDP structures

## Analytical Frame

Unit cost is a necessary variable, not a sufficient explanation.

A pricing-only approach misses three high-impact design layers:

- tier placement,
- utilization controls,
- channel and pharmacy pathway design.

## Framework for Interpretation

### Step 1: Establish Same-NDC Price Trend

Track matched NDC unit cost change across periods to avoid composition bias.

### Step 2: Overlay Access Design

Map concurrent changes in tier, PA/ST/QL, and network channel indicators.

### Step 3: Segment Plan Cohorts

Compare MA-PD and PDP responses separately before aggregating conclusions.

### Step 4: Test Price-Access Divergence

Flag cases where pricing is flat or down while access burden rises.

## Illustrative Pattern

| Signal | Example Direction | Interpretation Risk if Isolated |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Same-NDC Unit Price | Flat to modest decline | Suggests improving economics |
| Tier Position | Shift to higher tiers | Indicates worsening member pathway |
| PA / Step Therapy | Increased | Raises administrative friction |
| Channel Restrictions | More preferred-mail steering | Alters fulfillment pathway and persistence |

When these move in opposite directions, pricing trend alone becomes a weak proxy for practical access.

## Commercial Use Cases

- Loss-of-exclusivity planning where apparent price stability masks access tightening
- Pull-through diagnostics when coverage remains high but fill dynamics weaken
- Rebate and contracting strategy reviews where formulary outcomes diverge from expected price signals

### Segment Pattern Snapshot

| Plan Segment | Price Trend | Preferred Tier Trend | UM Trend | Net Access Assessment |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| MA-PD | +0.8% | Down | Up | Worsening |
| PDP | +0.3% | Flat | Slight up | Mixed |

## Interpretation Guardrails

- Do not infer intent from observed files.
- Do not treat average unit cost as patient cost.
- Do not collapse MA-PD and PDP dynamics into a single strategic narrative.

## Bottom Line

Unit cost data is most valuable when integrated with formulary, control, and channel signals. Pricing describes one dimension of plan behavior; access design describes the rest.
