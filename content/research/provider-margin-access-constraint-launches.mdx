## Objective

This research examines why buy-and-bill launches often stall even when there is visible demand and favorable clinical positioning.

## Core Question

Where does provider margin break, and what must change for adoption to become operationally acceptable?

## Analytical Lens

The most important variables are usually not abstract market size assumptions.

They are the mechanics that determine whether a provider can carry the product:

- net acquisition cost,
- reimbursement lag,
- 340B participation,
- white-bagging risk,
- site-of-care economics,
- claim friction and cash exposure.

## Interpretation

The access constraint is not always the payer.

In many launches, the provider is the binding constraint because the economics of stocking, administering, and collecting are not yet stable enough to support routine use.

## Commercial Implications

This type of analysis supports decisions about:

1. launch sequencing,
2. channel design,
3. Gross-to-Net assumptions,
4. reimbursement support materials,
5. field messaging around adoption risk.

## Bottom Line

If provider margin is negative or uncertain, access friction will surface before the market has a chance to normalize the product.
