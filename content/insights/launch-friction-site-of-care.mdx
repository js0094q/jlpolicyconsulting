The hardest launch problems rarely come from the headline access decision.

They come from the operational layers around it, especially where coding, reimbursement timing, and site-of-care economics collide.

## Where friction appears

The operational problems are usually familiar once you look for them:

- provider margin gets compressed before adoption starts,
- coding and billing mechanics slow reimbursement recognition,
- site-of-care economics make the preferred channel harder to use,
- cash exposure makes even covered products feel risky to stock.

None of those issues are abstract. They are the reasons a favorable coverage signal can still produce hesitation in the field.

## Better diagnostic

Instead of asking only whether a product is covered, ask:

1. Can the provider administer it without margin stress?
2. Can the claim clear with acceptable speed and reliability?
3. Does the site of care support the economics of use?

<Callout title="Important distinction">
Coverage answers whether the product can be used in theory.
Launch friction asks whether the pathway is stable enough for routine use in practice.
</Callout>
