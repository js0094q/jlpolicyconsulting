Tier movement is often tracked as a reporting field, but the current Part D transition environment makes that approach insufficient. When tier changes are interpreted with comparator windows and pathway variables, they become early signals of where access pressure may rise next. [1][3][4][6]

## Metadata

- Publish date: 2026-03-06
- Category: PBM and Formulary Dynamics
- Focus period: 2025Q4 to 2026Q1, with comparator context from 2025Q3 to 2025Q4
- Scope: Medicare Part D formulary-linked National Drug Code (NDC) observations across tier strata
- Evidence base: JL Policy SPUF outputs plus CMS, MedPAC, and KFF context anchors [1][2][3][4][5][6][7][8][9]

## Executive Summary

Observed fact: tier-linked footprint declined from `19,707,427` rows in 2025Q4 to `18,898,125` in 2026Q1 (`-4.11%`), while summed plan-count observations rose from `28,149` to `28,396` (`+247`). [1] Footprint contraction therefore did not come from a broad decline in observed plan-count volume.

Observed fact: Tier 1 linked rows fell by `469,640` (`-14.78%`). In the same window, mean deductible-application shares increased in Tier 3 to Tier 5 by `16.3211`, `18.5216`, and `18.6658` percentage points. [1]

Observed fact: broader transition composition was far less continuity-heavy than in the prior quarter pair, with matched-row share at `59.81%` and add/drop share at `40.19%` in `2025Q4 to 2026Q1`. [3][4]

Interpretation: tier movement should be treated as a decision signal only after decomposition and pathway checks. Without that, teams can overread one-quarter shifts or misattribute mechanism.

## Key Takeaways

- Tier movement requires comparator context and decomposition, not one-period interpretation. [3][4]
- The combination of Tier 1 contraction and higher-tier deductible-share increases indicates potential access burden migration inside covered benefit pathways. [1]
- Product-level worsening with mixed same-NDC quarter price movement shows that price-only narratives can fail. [2]
- Sponsor behavior is distributed across repricing, churn, and mixed patterns, so account-level segmentation is necessary. [5]
- Analytical discipline requires explicit separation of observed fact from interpretation.

## Main Analysis

### 1) What tier movement can show, and what it cannot

Observed fact: CMS Part D file architecture permits longitudinal tracking of tier placement and related pathway controls. [6] This makes tier movement measurable across plans and time.

Interpretation: tier movement is a directional signal, not a causal explanation. A single movement can reflect routine maintenance, class strategy, contracting dynamics, or broader benefit design responses. Teams should avoid single-variable narratives.

### 2) Comparator discipline changes the strategic reading

Observed fact: `2025Q3 to 2025Q4` was continuity-heavy, while `2025Q4 to 2026Q1` showed much higher composition churn. [3][4]

Interpretation: when continuity falls this sharply, one-quarter tier counts cannot be interpreted as pure within-entity behavior. Decomposition is required before assigning mechanism or deciding response intensity.

### 3) The strongest signal is the combined pathway pattern

Observed fact: Tier 1 linked rows declined significantly, while Tier 3 to Tier 5 deductible-application shares rose materially. [1]

Interpretation: this combined pattern is more informative than any single metric. It is consistent with a pathway where practical access burden can rise through deductible and tier structure, even when a product remains nominally covered.

### 4) Product-level examples show why price-only explanations are incomplete

Observed fact: the local top-25 worsening table includes products with sizable net higher-tier movement and mixed same-NDC quarter price changes. Examples include hydroxyzine pamoate (`+30.77%` net higher-tier share, `-16.44%` same-NDC quarter price change), nortriptyline hydrochloride (`+23.64%`, `+0.72%`), and dexmethylphenidate hydrochloride (`+6.77%`, `-0.60%`). [2]

Interpretation: at least in these observations, tier worsening cannot be reduced to same-NDC quarter price progression. Class management and benefit design conditions likely contribute.

### 5) Sponsor heterogeneity should shape operating governance

Observed fact: sponsor segmentation counts are dispersed across repricing-dominant (`185`), churn-dominant (`111`), mixed-high-activity (`110`), and mixed-moderate (`20`) groups. [5]

Interpretation: account planning should map sponsors to behavior clusters and set differentiated monitoring cadence. A single national average can obscure meaningful risk differences.

### 6) What is established versus inferred

Established by the cited evidence:
- linked footprint contraction with modest growth in observed plan-count rows, [1]
- Tier 1 decline plus higher-tier deductible-share increases, [1]
- less continuity-heavy composition in the transition pair, [3][4]
- product examples where tier worsening coexists with mixed same-NDC price movement. [2]

Not established by this evidence alone:
- uniform sponsor intent,
- direct causal effect size on patient-level utilization outcomes,
- complete contract-level net economics.

Interpretation: this is strategy-grade surveillance evidence that should trigger targeted account and class audits.

## Why It Matters

- Manufacturers: tier and deductible-pathway monitoring should be embedded in forecast governance, because preferred-position erosion can weaken pull-through before volume shifts are obvious. [1][2]
- Market access and reimbursement teams: deployment plans should combine decomposition metrics with sponsor segmentation, not a single blended trend line. [3][4][5]
- Policy and government affairs teams: redesign evaluation should track both affordability goals and access architecture outcomes. [7][8][9]
- Commercial leaders: reporting standards should require explicit fact-versus-interpretation labeling to reduce decision noise.

## Conclusion

Formulary tier movement is not a reporting artifact in the current Part D environment. It is an early indicator that helps identify where access pathway pressure may be building.

The decision-useful approach is integrated: compare windows, separate churn from repricing, track tier-linked footprint, and evaluate deductible pathway shifts. When teams apply that method, tier movement becomes actionable evidence instead of descriptive noise.
