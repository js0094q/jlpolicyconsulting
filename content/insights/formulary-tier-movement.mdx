Tier movement is often treated as descriptive.

In practice, it is measurable.

## What the Data Captures

CMS Part D files provide:

- plan-level tier placement,
- utilization management indicators,
- cost-sharing structures,
- pharmacy network design.

This allows tier movement to be evaluated alongside **control intensity and access conditions**, not in isolation.

## When Tier Movement Becomes Signal

Tier movement becomes actionable when three conditions are met:

1. **Repeatability**  
   Movement appears across multiple plans or persists across cycles.
2. **Concentration**  
   Movement is clustered within a class or plan segment.
3. **Overlay**  
   Movement coincides with tighter utilization or channel controls.

## Example: Generic Tier Worsening Pattern

Recent CMS-based analysis shows multiple mature generics experiencing:

- sustained higher-tier movement,
- minimal same-NDC unit price change,
- recurrence across plan cohorts.

This meets all three conditions above, making it a structural signal, not noise.

## What Tier Movement Alone Cannot Tell You

Tier movement does not explain:

- why the change occurred,
- whether it is permanent,
- or which lever is driving the shift.

It must be interpreted alongside:

- PA and step therapy,
- channel design,
- cost-sharing structure.

## Practical Interpretation Framework

When a product moves tiers:

1. Check class-level behavior.  
2. Overlay utilization controls.  
3. Segment by plan type.  
4. Evaluate channel and cost pathway.

This converts a descriptive change into a diagnostic workflow.

## Bottom Line

Tier movement is not the answer.

It is the first observable signal that something in the access system has changed.
