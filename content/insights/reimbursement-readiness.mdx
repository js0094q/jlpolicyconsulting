Clinical value matters.
It is not sufficient.

That is the gap many launch plans miss.

## What Changes the Decision

A product is reimbursement-ready when the people who have to use it can actually absorb it.
That means the launch has to work across:

- provider margin,
- payer controls,
- channel design,
- and the patient cost pathway.

If those pieces point in different directions, the product may be covered and still not be easy to use.

## Where Friction Shows Up

The practical friction points are usually not abstract:

- tier placement can worsen the patient path,
- utilization management can slow prescribing,
- channel restrictions can shift fulfillment away from the most workable site of care,
- and reimbursement lag can push the cost burden onto the provider.

Those are commercial variables, not just policy variables.

## The Wrong Question

The wrong question is whether the product is covered.

The better question is whether the access pathway is operationally tolerable for the party that has to carry the cost first.

## Launch Implication

When reimbursement readiness is weak, manufacturers usually see the same pattern:

- slower pull-through,
- more conditional prescribing,
- and greater dependence on field-level explanation of economics.

That is why reimbursement readiness should be treated as a launch input, not a post-launch diagnosis.

## Bottom Line

Clinical value opens the door.
Reimbursement readiness decides whether the product can walk through it.
