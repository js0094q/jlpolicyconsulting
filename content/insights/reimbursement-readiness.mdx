Clinical value matters, but it does not create reimbursement readiness.

Reimbursement readiness is the operational test. It asks whether the product can move through provider, payer, and channel mechanics without creating avoidable cost, delay, or administrative burden.

## Reimbursement readiness is an operational test

A product is reimbursement-ready when the party that orders, dispenses, or administers it can absorb the cost, timing, and administrative burden. That means the launch has to work across:

- provider margin,
- payer controls,
- channel design,
- and the patient cost pathway.

If those pieces point in different directions, the product may be covered and still not be easy to use.

## Where friction shows up

The practical friction points are usually not abstract:

- tier placement can worsen the patient path,
- utilization management can slow prescribing,
- channel restrictions can shift fulfillment away from the most workable site of care,
- reimbursement lag can push the cost burden onto the provider.

Those are commercial variables, not just policy variables.

The error is to treat them as downstream implementation details. In practice, they shape whether the launch is easy to explain, easy to prescribe, and easy to keep moving.

## The wrong question

The wrong question is whether the product is covered.

The better question is whether the access pathway is operationally tolerable for the party that has to carry the cost first.
