Between `2025Q4` and `2026Q1`, a matched SPUF analysis identified a repeated pattern: tier placement worsened for commoditized generics even when same-NDC unit pricing was often stable.

## What We Measured

The analysis linked plan, formulary, and pricing records at matched `plan + NDC (+ days supply)` grain, then rolled results to product concept level.

## Headline Findings

- All `29 of 29` products in the commoditized counter-story set showed positive net movement into higher tiers.
- Median net higher-tier share increase was `4.13 percentage points`.
- `16 of 29` products had same-NDC unit-cost change of `+2%` or less and still moved net-higher in tier share.
- `12 of 29` products had same-NDC unit costs decline quarter-over-quarter and still moved net-higher in tier share.

| gentamicin sulfate | -0.1% | +4.7 pp |

## Why This Matters for Access Teams

Price trend alone can miss changes in practical access. In this quarter transition, tier design movement appears to be a meaningful pathway for burden shift even when matched unit-cost signals are muted.

## Caveats

- This is a quarter-pair snapshot, not a long-run trend model.
- Tier movement is an access-friction signal, not a direct out-of-pocket estimate.
- Generic classification and matching rely on source mapping quality and quarter comparability constraints.
