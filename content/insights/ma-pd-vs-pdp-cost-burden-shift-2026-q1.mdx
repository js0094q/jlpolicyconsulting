In the `2025Q4` to `2026Q1` transition, matched SPUF observations show a clear average split between MA-PD and PDP on burden-related signals.

## Headline Segment Differences

- Matched observations: MA-PD `12,309,773`; PDP `1,107,605`.
- Share moving up tier: MA-PD `5.04%`; PDP `2.36%` (difference `+2.68 pp`, 95% CI `2.65 to 2.71 pp`).
- Share with cost worsening: MA-PD `43.64%`; PDP `35.30%` (difference `+8.34 pp`, 95% CI `8.25 to 8.44 pp`).
- Net tier worsening: MA-PD `+2.10 pp`; PDP `-0.73 pp`.
- Burden worsened composite: MA-PD `44.65%`; PDP `35.44%`.

## Product Dispersion Behind the Averages

Across overlapping product rows, MA-PD showed:

- Higher up-tier rates in `87.1%` of overlap products.
- Higher cost-worsening rates in `56.2%` of overlap products.
- Higher burden-worsened rates in `57.2%` of overlap products.

The segment effect is directional, but not uniform. Product-level outcomes still vary materially.

## Sponsor Dispersion Matters

MA-PD sponsor outcomes were widely distributed, including large variation among high-volume sponsors. That pattern supports using segment-level benchmarks for triage, then sponsor-level prioritization for execution.

## Why This Matters Commercially

Treating MA-PD and PDP as one blended market can hide practical risk. Forecasting and contracting decisions are generally stronger when segment baselines are separated before portfolio roll-up.

## Caveats

- Plan type mapping uses contract prefix conventions (`H/R` for MA-PD, `S` for PDP).
- Therapeutic class breakout is limited by available source fields.
- Some product-level extremes may reflect small-denominator effects and should be threshold-filtered before executive use.
