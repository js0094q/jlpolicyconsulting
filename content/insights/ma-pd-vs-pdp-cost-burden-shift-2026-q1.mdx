## Metadata

- Period analyzed: `2025Q3 to 2025Q4` and `2025Q4 to 2026Q1`
- Segment definition: Medicare Advantage Prescription Drug plans (`MA-PD`) and stand-alone Prescription Drug Plans (`PDP`) using contract prefix mapping (`H/R` for MA-PD, `S` for PDP)
- Data grain: contract-level decomposition from published repricing-versus-churn scorecards
- Primary question: where did quarter-transition pressure show up by segment, and what should be interpreted as composition change versus repricing behavior?

## Executive Summary

Part D commentary often treats MA-PD and PDP as one blended market. That shortcut is risky in high-churn transitions. In the `2025Q4 to 2026Q1` window, both segments showed very high repricing share among matched rows, but MA-PD carried higher churn share of total rows (`40.76%`) than PDP (`32.04%`). In the prior `2025Q3 to 2025Q4` window, churn share was low and similar in both segments (`5.54%` MA-PD, `5.28%` PDP). [4][5]

The operational implication is straightforward: the later window is still useful, but it is composition-sensitive, especially in MA-PD. Teams should not read headline quarter-over-quarter deltas as pure same-product repricing when adds and drops are this large. [4][5]

This does not mean MA-PD is always "worse" or PDP is always "cleaner." It means inferential quality changed sharply between the two windows, and that quality shift was larger in MA-PD. [4][5][6]

## Key Takeaways

- MA-PD and PDP both showed high matched-row repricing in `2025Q4 to 2026Q1`, but MA-PD had higher composition churn. [5]
- Compared with `2025Q3 to 2025Q4`, churn intensity increased much more than normal in both segments, with the larger jump in MA-PD. [4][5]
- A decomposition-first read is required before claiming segment-level access tightening or pricing strategy shifts. [4][6]
- The useful workflow is: pair-quality check, then same-product checks, then segment interpretation. [4][5]
- Segment-level differences should inform account prioritization, not be collapsed into one national narrative. [5]

## Main Analysis

### 1) What is being observed

The observed pattern is not just "more repricing." The observed pattern is a major shift in pair quality between windows, especially for MA-PD, where add/drop churn rose from `5.54%` to `40.76%` of rows. PDP also moved to a churn-heavy profile, but less sharply (`5.28%` to `32.04%`). [4][5]

That is a concrete change in what the data can support. In low-churn windows, quarter-over-quarter interpretation is more stable at matched-row grain. In high-churn windows, composition effects are large enough to distort simple headline comparisons. [4][5][6]

### 2) Where it is showing up and compared with what

It is showing up in both plan-type segments, but with different magnitudes. Relative to the prior window:

- MA-PD churn share increased by `+35.22` percentage points.
- PDP churn share increased by `+26.76` percentage points.
- MA-PD matched-row coverage fell from `49.9M` to `37.8M` despite higher total rows.
- PDP matched-row coverage also fell, from `4.17M` to `3.03M`. [4][5]

So the right comparator is not only MA-PD versus PDP within one quarter pair. It is each segment versus its own baseline in the prior continuity-rich pair. [4][5]

### 3) What is driving the pattern

The first driver is composition churn, not a single uniform repricing mechanism. High adds and drops mechanically change who is in the panel before any interpretation about persistent same-product movement is made. [4][5]

The second driver is segment structure. MA-PD and PDP are different business environments, so identical policy timing does not require identical observed transition behavior. That is why segment separation is a measurement requirement, not a stylistic preference. [2][3][5]

### 4) What this does and does not prove

This evidence supports a decomposition claim: the `2025Q4 to 2026Q1` transition is composition-sensitive in both segments and more so in MA-PD. [5]

It does not, on its own, prove a uniform strategic intent across sponsors or classes. Further sponsor-level and product-level controls are still required before any intent-level narrative is publishable. [5][6]

## Why It Matters

- Manufacturers: segment-specific access and pricing surveillance should be separated before portfolio conclusions are presented to leadership. [5]
- Market access and reimbursement teams: account prioritization should weight churn-adjusted signals, not only raw quarter deltas. [4][5]
- Policy and government affairs teams: post-redesign monitoring should track where management pressure appears by segment, not only in aggregate averages. [2][3]
- Commercialization leaders: forecast error risk rises when high-churn windows are interpreted as stable trend windows. [4][5][6]

## Conclusion

The `2025Q4 to 2026Q1` transition should not be read as a simple repricing story for either MA-PD or PDP. It is a churn-heavy transition with materially different segment intensity.

A serious reading sequence is: check pair quality, isolate matched behavior, then interpret segment patterns. If that order is skipped, teams can mistake composition turnover for stable strategic movement.
