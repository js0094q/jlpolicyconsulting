# Interview-Ready Talking Points (NDA-Safe)

## 60-Second Version
My work is reimbursement-first commercialization, but the broader point is that I operate across the full market access surface area. I map how an asset behaves across IPPS, NTAP, HOPPS/OPPS, PFS, Medicaid, MA, MA-PD, commercial, Part D, and 340B, then convert that into account-level economics, launch decisions, and in-market management. The key insight is consistent: clinical potential alone does not determine uptake when provider margin, coding visibility, timing lag, formulary control, and channel mix are misaligned. I separate structural constraints from recoverable leakage, then use coding, contracting, channel design, and field reimbursement execution to recover value. That approach is transferable across assets, and it is especially useful where reimbursement, GTN, and lifecycle uncertainty are high.

## 2-Minute Version
I approach market access as an operating system, not a messaging exercise. In complex launches, the strategic risk is rarely just whether payers cover the product. It is whether the reimbursement pathway, site-of-care economics, and channel structure let providers use it without unsustainable margin pressure, and whether the same logic still holds once the product is in market.

My work starts by mapping the payment architecture across inpatient, outpatient, office, pharmacy benefit, and channel-specific settings. In inpatient contexts, the issue is often bundled payment exposure with transitional support that reduces but does not eliminate risk. In outpatient settings, pass-through or transitional payment can create temporary economics that later reset. In office settings, ASP-linked timing can create spread pressure in buy-and-bill channels. Add 340B and the picture becomes mix-sensitive: utilization can grow while realized net weakens. For mid-cycle products, I also look at formulary and Part D controls because pharmacy-benefit access can move independently of the medical benefit.

From there, I quantify GTN pressure buckets, segment accounts by economics and adoption readiness, and run scenario stress-tests for launch, in-market, and post-transition states. Then I target recoverable losses using specific levers: coding strategy, channel architecture, contract design, reimbursement support precision, and evidence framing tied to utilization outcomes. The output is practical: field tools, KPIs, and governance that align policy mechanics with commercial performance.

That is the level of work a head of market access needs: not just reimbursement expertise, but the ability to connect launch strategy, mature-brand management, payer behavior, provider economics, and field execution into one operating model.

## What I Would Assess On A New Asset

- Reimbursement pathway map by setting and channel.
- Site-of-care and site-of-service economics.
- Provider margin and collection timing.
- GTN leakage by channel, contract, and 340B exposure.
- Evidence gaps that matter to hospitals or payers.
- Launch-phase actions versus post-transition actions.

## Five Sharp Points For Panel Q&A
1. Reimbursement architecture is behavior architecture; if you model it late, you lose value early.
2. Transitional payment support is a bridge, not a business model.
3. Mix quality matters: where volume comes from can matter more than total volume.
4. Distinguish structural economics from execution leakage before negotiating concessions.
5. Access strategy works best when evidence is linked to measurable utilization and workflow outcomes.
