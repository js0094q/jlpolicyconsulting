# Contact Page Spec
## JL Policy Consulting Website

## Purpose

The Contact page exists to make outreach easy, credible, and low-friction.

Its job is to:
- give visitors a clear path to get in touch,
- support consulting-related outreach,
- maintain the site’s professional and restrained tone,
- avoid unnecessary friction or over-explanation.

This page should feel simple, trustworthy, and direct.

---

## Primary Audience

Primary audiences include:
- prospective clients,
- strategic partners,
- industry contacts,
- people reaching out about consulting or analysis work.

---

## Primary User Questions to Answer

1. How do I get in touch?
2. What kinds of outreach is this page intended for?
3. Is this a serious, professional contact pathway?

---

## Primary Conversion Goal

Primary CTA:
- submit contact inquiry
- or send email directly

The page should reduce friction around outreach.

---

## Required Tone

The Contact page must feel:
- direct,
- clean,
- professional,
- low-friction,
- credible.

It must not feel:
- overbuilt,
- salesy,
- cluttered,
- performative.

---

## Required Page Structure

Use this structure:

1. Header
2. Contact Intro
3. Contact Method / Form
4. Optional Short Context Section
5. Footer

---

## Section Requirements

### 1. Contact Intro
Must include:
- clear page title,
- short supportive copy.

The intro should explain that the page is for consulting, strategic, and professional inquiries.

Keep it concise.

### 2. Contact Method / Form
This is the main functional section.

It should provide one clear contact path:
- email link,
- contact form,
- or both.

If using a form, keep required fields minimal.

Recommended fields:
- name
- email
- organization, optional
- message

Do not create a long intake form unless there is a very clear reason.

### 3. Optional Short Context Section
If useful, include a short section indicating what kinds of inquiries are appropriate, such as:
- consulting engagements,
- strategic analysis,
- collaboration or professional contact.

Keep this short and calm.

---

## Copy Rules

The Contact page should:
- be concise,
- reduce hesitation,
- avoid marketing-heavy CTA language,
- maintain the site’s authority and professionalism.

Avoid:
- long persuasive copy,
- excessive reassurance language,
- overcomplicated process explanations,
- aggressive lead-gen tactics.

---

## Design Rules

The Contact page should:
- feel clean and simple,
- use controlled spacing,
- keep the contact path visually obvious,
- avoid decorative complexity.

Do not:
- bury the form or email path,
- overload the page with extra content,
- make the page feel like a landing page.

---

## Success Criteria

The Contact page is successful if:
- the visitor immediately understands how to reach out,
- the page feels trustworthy and professional,
- the pathway to contact is low-friction,
- the page remains visually consistent with the rest of the site.
