# Consulting Page Spec
## JL Policy Consulting Website

## Purpose

The Consulting page is the primary services and offer page.

Its job is to:
- explain what kinds of advisory work JL Policy Consulting provides,
- organize the work into clear strategic categories,
- show the kinds of problems and decisions the work supports,
- help qualified visitors understand whether the consultancy is relevant to their needs.

This page should convert interest into serious evaluation.

---

## Primary Audience

Primary audiences include:
- manufacturers,
- market access and reimbursement teams,
- policy and government affairs teams,
- commercialization teams,
- stakeholders needing reimbursement or policy strategy support.

---

## Primary User Questions to Answer

1. What kinds of consulting work are offered?
2. What problems does each area address?
3. Who is this work for?
4. What decisions or business questions does this work inform?
5. How should I engage further?

---

## Primary Conversion Goal

Primary CTA:
- `Contact Us`

This page should convert qualified interest into outreach.

---

## Secondary Conversion Goals

Secondary CTAs may include:
- `Read Insights`
- `Explore Research`

These should support authority, not compete with contact.

---

## Required Tone

The Consulting page must feel:
- concrete,
- commercially credible,
- structured,
- high-signal,
- decision-oriented.

It must not feel:
- vague,
- brochure-like,
- stuffed with capability phrases,
- generic.

---

## Required Page Structure

Use this structure:

1. Header
2. Consulting Hero
3. Core Consulting Overview
4. Consulting Areas
5. How the Work Creates Value
6. Engagement / Work Product Framing
7. Proof / Credibility
8. CTA
9. Footer

---

## Section Requirements

### 1. Consulting Hero
Must include:
- concise headline,
- short body copy,
- primary CTA,
- optional supporting proof or orientation panel.

The hero should clarify that the page is about strategic advisory work, not just expertise topics.

### 2. Core Consulting Overview
This section should explain the consultancy’s overall role.

It should define the broad type of support provided, for example:
- reimbursement strategy,
- policy interpretation,
- market access analysis,
- payer and provider dynamics,
- data-driven strategic support.

This section should set the frame before diving into service areas.

### 3. Consulting Areas
This is the most important section on the page.

Use 3 to 5 structured service blocks.

Each consulting area should answer:
- what it is,
- what issue it addresses,
- who it is for,
- what decisions it informs.

Recommended themes:
- Reimbursement Strategy
- Medicare and Drug Pricing Policy
- Market Access and Payer Dynamics
- Biosimilars and Commercialization Strategy
- Data-Driven Policy and Access Analysis

### 4. How the Work Creates Value
This section should explain why the work matters.

Possible themes:
- translating policy changes into strategic implications,
- clarifying access risk and opportunity,
- informing launch and in-market decisions,
- connecting reimbursement mechanics to commercial execution.

This section should not repeat service names. It should explain practical value.

### 5. Engagement / Work Product Framing
This section should explain the nature of deliverables or support.

Examples may include:
- strategic analysis,
- policy interpretation,
- issue briefs,
- quantitative analysis,
- decision support tools,
- market access framing.

Keep this concise and professional.

### 6. Proof / Credibility
Proof can include:
- domain depth,
- policy and reimbursement experience,
- analytical rigor,
- cross-functional relevance.

Keep this trust-building, not self-congratulatory.

### 7. CTA
Preferred CTA:
- `Contact Us`

The CTA should be direct and easy to act on.

---

## Copy Rules

Consulting copy must:
- be specific,
- explain real problems,
- sound useful to sophisticated stakeholders,
- connect expertise to decisions.

Avoid:
- list-only capabilities,
- inflated advisory language,
- jargon without purpose,
- repeating the same value proposition in every block.

---

## Design Rules

The Consulting page should:
- use clear section hierarchy,
- use card/group structures for consulting areas,
- keep typography controlled,
- feel more structured than the homepage.

Do not:
- present consulting areas as a flat tag list,
- overload the page with long text walls,
- let all sections feel equal in weight.

---

## Success Criteria

The Consulting page is successful if:
- the consulting offer is easy to understand,
- the work feels concrete and relevant,
- the service structure is more useful than a capabilities list,
- the page supports a strong contact conversion path.
