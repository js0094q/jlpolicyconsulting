# Homepage Spec
## JL Policy Consulting Website

## Purpose

The homepage is the primary authority and conversion page for the website.

Its job is to:
- explain who JL Policy Consulting serves,
- explain what kinds of problems the firm helps solve,
- establish differentiated credibility,
- direct visitors into Consulting, Insights, Research, or Contact,
- create a strong first impression of analytical rigor and professional seriousness.

The homepage is not a general brochure and should not try to explain everything.

---

## Primary Audience

Primary audiences include:
- pharmaceutical manufacturers,
- market access and reimbursement leaders,
- policy and government affairs stakeholders,
- commercialization teams,
- payer-facing strategy teams,
- partners evaluating strategic advisory support.

Secondary audiences include:
- broader healthcare stakeholders,
- recruiters or professional contacts,
- readers discovering the site through research or insights content.

---

## Primary User Questions to Answer

The homepage must answer these questions quickly:

1. What is JL Policy Consulting?
2. What kinds of issues does the firm help address?
3. Why is this expertise different from generic policy or consulting language?
4. What should I do next if this is relevant to my work?

---

## Primary Conversion Goal

Primary CTA:
- `View Consulting`

The homepage should primarily move qualified visitors toward understanding the consulting offer.

---

## Secondary Conversion Goals

Secondary CTAs may include:
- `Read Insights`
- `Explore Research`
- `Contact Us`

These should support the main conversion path without competing with it.

---

## Required Tone

The homepage must feel:
- authoritative,
- restrained,
- specific,
- commercially credible,
- policy-literate,
- premium.

It must not feel:
- generic,
- sales-heavy,
- inflated,
- oversized,
- cluttered,
- trendy.

---

## Required Page Structure

The homepage should follow this order:

1. Header
2. Hero
3. What I Help Solve
4. Why This Perspective Is Different
5. Selected Consulting Areas
6. Proof / Credibility Strip
7. Featured Insight + Featured Research
8. Closing CTA
9. Footer

Do not materially deviate from this sequence unless there is a clear strategic reason.

---

## Section Requirements

### 1. Header
Must include:
- JL Policy Consulting brand/wordmark
- navigation to About, Consulting, Insights, Research, Contact

Header should be compact and visually restrained.

### 2. Hero
Must include:
- short eyebrow
- concise headline
- short supporting paragraph
- primary CTA
- secondary CTA
- optional compact proof panel or focus panel

Hero must clearly communicate:
- who the site serves,
- what kind of work is done,
- why it matters.

Hero must not:
- be oversized,
- use long generic copy,
- dominate the page height,
- attempt to describe every capability.

### 3. What I Help Solve
This section should summarize major problem areas in 3 to 4 structured cards.

Recommended themes:
- Medicare reimbursement and policy impact
- Drug pricing and gross-to-net strategy
- PBM, formulary, and payer dynamics
- Biosimilars, generics, and commercialization economics

Each card should explain:
- the problem,
- why it matters,
- how the work helps decision-making.

### 4. Why This Perspective Is Different
Must explain the difference between:
- generic policy commentary,
- commercially useful reimbursement and market access analysis.

This section should establish that the work connects policy, coding, payment, payer dynamics, provider economics, and launch/commercial implications.

### 5. Selected Consulting Areas
Must present 3 to 4 consulting area blocks.

Each block should answer:
- what the work is,
- who it is for,
- what decisions it informs.

This section should feel more concrete than the earlier “What I Help Solve” section.

### 6. Proof / Credibility Strip
Must introduce proof before the page gets too deep.

Acceptable proof includes:
- years of experience,
- type of strategic experience,
- authority themes,
- analytical orientation,
- selected metrics if used with restraint.

This section should support trust, not feel like a résumé dump.

### 7. Featured Insight + Featured Research
Must include:
- 1 featured insight,
- 1 featured research item,
- concise framing for the editorial/research angle.

This section should show authority content without overwhelming the homepage.

### 8. Closing CTA
Must provide a clean invitation to engage.

Preferred CTA:
- `Contact Us`

This should be direct, restrained, and clear.

### 9. Footer
Must include:
- brand
- key nav
- contact email
- LinkedIn
- copyright

Footer should remain secondary and compact.

---

## Copy Rules

Homepage copy must:
- be concise,
- be specific,
- avoid generic consulting language,
- communicate strategic and analytical seriousness,
- connect policy and reimbursement topics to real-world decisions.

Avoid:
- “thought leadership” language,
- broad mission statements,
- vague advisory phrasing,
- filler paragraphs,
- repeated claims across multiple sections.

---

## Design Rules

Homepage design must:
- maintain restrained premium styling,
- use controlled typography,
- avoid oversized headings,
- maintain strong hierarchy,
- avoid content sprawl,
- feel calm and highly intentional.

The homepage must not:
- feel like a landing page template,
- rely on scale for impact,
- use too many equal-weight sections.

---

## Success Criteria

The homepage is successful if:
- the first screen is materially sharper and tighter than the old structure,
- the value proposition is easy to understand,
- credibility appears early,
- the consulting pathway is clear,
- authority content supports rather than distracts,
- the page feels premium, analytical, and conversion-oriented.
