# About Page Spec
## JL Policy Consulting Website

## Purpose

The About page exists to build trust.

Its job is to:
- explain the professional perspective behind JL Policy Consulting,
- establish credibility without reading like a résumé,
- show how experience translates into useful client-facing work,
- reinforce the site’s authority and seriousness.

This page should make the visitor feel that the expertise is real, specific, and commercially relevant.

---

## Primary Audience

Primary audiences include:
- prospective clients,
- strategic partners,
- manufacturer and policy stakeholders,
- decision-makers evaluating whether the perspective is credible and relevant.

Secondary audiences include:
- recruiters,
- industry contacts,
- readers seeking context on the firm’s expertise.

---

## Primary User Questions to Answer

1. Who is behind JL Policy Consulting?
2. Why is this expertise credible?
3. What makes this perspective useful in practice?
4. How does this experience translate into the work offered on the site?

---

## Primary Conversion Goal

Primary CTA:
- `View Consulting`

The About page should move readers toward understanding the consulting offer.

---

## Secondary Conversion Goal

Secondary CTA:
- `Contact Us`

---

## Required Tone

The About page must feel:
- credible,
- direct,
- mature,
- analytical,
- high-trust.

It must not feel:
- self-promotional,
- bloated,
- biographical for its own sake,
- generic,
- overly personal.

---

## Required Page Structure

Use this structure:

1. Header
2. Intro / About Hero
3. Professional Positioning
4. Experience and Perspective
5. How the Work Is Approached
6. Selected Expertise Themes
7. Proof / Credibility
8. CTA
9. Footer

---

## Section Requirements

### 1. Intro / About Hero
Must include:
- concise headline,
- short explanatory paragraph,
- optional short subheading or eyebrow.

This section should establish who the page is about and what perspective is being offered.

Do not use:
- long biography-style copy,
- oversized personal branding language.

### 2. Professional Positioning
This section should define the core professional identity in strategic terms.

It should explain the domain focus, such as:
- health policy,
- reimbursement,
- market access,
- drug pricing,
- payer/provider dynamics,
- healthcare data analysis.

This section should connect background to present value.

### 3. Experience and Perspective
This section should summarize relevant professional experience selectively.

It should:
- highlight domain relevance,
- show range across policy and commercialization contexts,
- avoid turning into a full CV.

Focus on what strengthens trust.

### 4. How the Work Is Approached
This section should explain the analytical approach.

Examples:
- translating reimbursement mechanics into strategic implications,
- connecting policy change to launch or access decisions,
- grounding analysis in real operational and market behavior.

This should help the visitor understand how the thinking works.

### 5. Selected Expertise Themes
Use structured blocks or cards to show expertise areas.

Examples:
- Medicare reimbursement and policy
- drug pricing and gross-to-net
- PBM and formulary dynamics
- biosimilars and commercialization
- quantitative healthcare policy analysis

### 6. Proof / Credibility
Proof may include:
- years of experience,
- types of work delivered,
- strategic domains covered,
- policy and reimbursement areas worked in.

Keep this disciplined and relevant.

### 7. CTA
Preferred CTA:
- `View Consulting`
- optional secondary CTA: `Contact Us`

---

## Copy Rules

The About page should:
- explain the professional perspective,
- show why the expertise matters,
- avoid generic “about me” writing,
- avoid over-personalization,
- stay concise and high-signal.

Avoid:
- chronology-heavy writing,
- personal mission clichés,
- inflated leadership language,
- repeating the homepage word for word.

---

## Design Rules

The About page should:
- feel editorial and structured,
- prioritize readability,
- use controlled typography,
- use sectioning to organize trust-building content.

Do not:
- overdesign biography content,
- create oversized quote sections,
- make the page feel like a résumé pasted into the site.

---

## Success Criteria

The About page is successful if:
- the visitor understands who is behind the firm,
- the expertise feels credible and relevant,
- the page strengthens trust,
- the page avoids résumé sprawl,
- the page naturally supports movement into Consulting or Contact.
