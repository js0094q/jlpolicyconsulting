# Research Index Spec
## JL Policy Consulting Website

## Purpose

The Research index is the editorial and analytical listing page for deeper-form work.

Its job is to:
- present more substantive analysis clearly,
- distinguish research from shorter insights,
- reinforce analytical depth,
- support discovery of longer-form or more data-driven work.

This page should feel rigorous, structured, and credible.

---

## Primary Audience

Primary audiences include:
- readers seeking deeper analysis,
- prospective clients evaluating analytical depth,
- stakeholders looking for structured research-driven content.

---

## Primary User Questions to Answer

1. What qualifies as “Research” on this site?
2. How is it different from Insights?
3. Does the work reflect analytical depth and seriousness?
4. Which items are most relevant to my interests?

---

## Primary Conversion Goal

Primary CTA:
- `Read Research`

---

## Secondary Conversion Goals

Secondary CTAs may include:
- `View Consulting`
- `Contact Us`

Use lightly.

---

## Required Tone

The Research index must feel:
- rigorous,
- calm,
- substantive,
- highly credible,
- editorial.

It must not feel:
- bloated,
- flashy,
- cluttered,
- like a generic blog listing.

---

## Required Page Structure

Use this structure:

1. Header
2. Research Intro
3. Optional Research Framing / Method Note
4. Research Grid / List
5. Optional Featured Research
6. Optional CTA Band
7. Footer

---

## Section Requirements

### 1. Research Intro
Must include:
- page title,
- concise explanation of what the Research section contains.

This should establish that Research is the deeper analytical layer of the site.

### 2. Optional Research Framing / Method Note
This section may explain the type of work featured here, such as:
- quantitative policy analysis,
- market dynamics analysis,
- reimbursement and access research,
- structured interpretation of healthcare datasets and policy shifts.

Keep this concise.

### 3. Research Grid / List
This is the main content block.

Each item should include:
- title,
- summary,
- publish date,
- category,
- optional tags,
- clear read action.

Research cards may carry slightly more weight than insight cards, but should remain consistent with site style.

### 4. Optional Featured Research
Use only if a flagship piece deserves emphasis.

Do not let featured modules dominate the entire page.

### 5. Optional CTA Band
May direct readers toward Consulting or Contact if relevant.

Keep restrained.

---

## Content Rules

Research content should remain distinct from Insights and should reflect the deeper-form side of the site’s content model. The site currently supports separate `content/research` and `content/insights` collections, which should remain editorially distinct. Avoid:

- using vague summaries,
- crowding the page with noise.

Prefer:
- stronger summaries,
- structured layout,

---

## Design Rules

The Research index should:
- feel slightly more substantive than Insights,
- preserve the site-wide style system,
- remain highly readable,
- use clean grid/list patterns,
- avoid visual heaviness.

---

## Success Criteria

The Research index is successful if:
- visitors understand that Research is the deeper analytical section,
- the page feels rigorous and distinct from Insights,
- the listing supports discovery without clutter,
- the page reinforces the site’s analytical credibility.
